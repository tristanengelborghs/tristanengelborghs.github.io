Featured · Vision-language-action

A 500M vision-language-action model on LIBERO-Goal

I turned the SmolVLM2-500M vision-language model into a robot policy. It reads two camera views and a natural-language instruction, then outputs a chunk of continuous arm actions. LoRA adapters on the language model's attention train 4.8M of its 465M parameters. Eight learned action queries appended to the sequence feed a small regression head, following the OpenVLA-OFT recipe inside my own benchmark harness. I compared it with a 27M ResNet-FiLM policy trained from scratch, on the same 500 initial states.

How it was measured

One NVIDIA L4 GPU, a pinned LIBERO commit, and 50 episodes per task for each model on the same initial states. The difference is tested with a paired sign-flip permutation test. Language ablations destroy the instruction at evaluation, or in training as well.

What it showed

On Goal, the instruction is nearly everything. Without it, both models fall to or below the 1-in-10 guess rate (5.2% and 0.4%). The VLA's gains come on the baseline's hardest tasks: pushing the plate goes from 72% to 100%. It loses ground on the two-stage drawer-and-bowl task. The from-scratch baseline already beats the published from-scratch reference (Diffusion Policy, about 68%), so pretraining bought six points for 4.6× the GPU-hours.

A bug worth finding

LIBERO spells four of the ten Goal instructions differently in the demonstration files and in the evaluator. The language-conditioned baseline had been training on one instruction embedding and rolling out on another. Training now reads each instruction exactly as the evaluator does.

Single training seed. I treat the numbers as preliminary until they replicate over at least three seeds.

MuJoCo · RL · Tactile

Dexterous in-hand manipulation

A Shadow Hand (24 joints, 20 actuators, tendon-coupled finger joints) reorients a cube through intermittent contact. I built the environment, domain randomisation and fingertip touch sensing, and a threaded simulator that runs about 1,700 control steps per second across 8 environments on an M1 laptop. On top sit a PPO teacher trained on privileged state with an adaptive curriculum, and a DAgger student that sees only proprioception, touch and the goal, with no cube pose.

Trained on a laptop: the whole pipeline and its learning signal are verified end to end. Full 3-D reorientation needs GPU-scale compute.

Computer vision · Imitation

Robot demonstrations from human video

This pipeline films a person doing a task and converts the video into robot demonstrations in LIBERO's format. It tracks the hand with MediaPipe, fits the palm pose with Kabsch alignment and smooths it with a 1-Euro filter that coasts through occlusions. The pose is then retargeted to end-effector actions resampled onto the robot's 20 Hz control clock.

Each stage guards against a specific silent failure, such as a per-frame handedness flip that yields a fully confident 180° error.

Learning from demonstration in Isaac Lab

Teaching a Franka arm to grasp and lift blocks from demonstrations. The pipeline covers keyboard and gamepad teleoperation, differential IK, HDF5 demos validated by replaying them to success, and diffusion-policy imitation alongside PPO. I drive everything from my laptop over ssh into a containerised Isaac Lab on a rented GPU, and patched three Isaac Lab 3.0.0 bugs along the way.

Research

Clinical machine learning

For people with drug-resistant epilepsy, surgery can stop seizures, but only if the region where they start is found and removed. My thesis builds machine learning around the intracranial recordings used to find it.

Master's thesis · KU Leuven · 2025–2026

Predicting epilepsy-surgery outcome from intracranial EEG

I built the machine-learning workflow for a two-centre clinical study end to end. It covers clinical data management, then preprocessing and quality control of intracranial recordings. Model development and evaluation used masked autoencoders, graph attention networks and attention-based multiple-instance learning. I also built a desktop research application for clinicians. It uploads recordings, runs inference and shows predictions in interactive 3-D, with saliency maps and electrode-level biomarker overlays such as high-frequency oscillations, phase–amplitude coupling and connectivity.

Follow-up research, ongoing

A reproducible pipeline turns raw recordings into 37 per-contact markers across 8 families, from spectral power and signal instability to cross-frequency coupling, directed connectivity, and reference methods such as the epileptogenicity index and neural fragility. Each family is paired with a surrogate manipulation that must abolish it. Every stage tracks its provenance, writes a QC report, and runs identically on a laptop or as SLURM array jobs on a compute cluster.

Side project

Restoring underwater video

As a dive instructor I film on the reef, where water scatters and absorbs light and footage turns flat and blue with depth.

Original Restored

Veil removal, then colour

Each frame gets veil (backscatter) removal in linear light, driven by a dark-channel estimate of how much light survived, then local contrast and light sharpening. A 3-D colour lookup table fitted to one AI-edited reference frame is then applied to the whole 4K clip with FFmpeg.

On held-out pixels the error to the reference fell from 0.160 to 0.055. The output-to-input brightness ratio moves by at most 0.007 between consecutive frames, so the grade doesn't flicker.
